Loring AFB was an air base built in the town of Limestone in northeastern Maine. It was shut down in the '90s after the Cold War ended. It had a lot of nukes stored there ready to be flown over to Moscow at any moment (fun times). This page is mostly to show many photos I have from exploring the place over my 2 years at MSSM (and afterward) which is a 20 minute bike ride away.

Shoutout Bill Buchholz from the Chickawaukie Ice Boat Club for letting me sail one of the Blokarts around on the runway. It was so much more fun than "softwater sailing" as they would call it.

Command Center

/images/loring/command_center.jpg

This is a bunker right next to the south end of the runway, on the east side. I don't have any good photos unfortunately. It is super creepy and should not be explored alone. There is a computer room with some leftover electronics, raised floor for cable routing, and a cooling system. We explored the basement at one point and found some gym equipment.

Train coal dumping station

/images/loring/coal_dump_side_view.JPG Coal Station from North /images/loring/coal_dump_from_away.jpg Coal Station from West /images/loring/coal_dump_from_side_arm_base.jpg Arm of the Tower

In the summer of 2026 I explored the base with my friend Brenden who guaduated MSSM sometime around 2000 (+/-). We spent a long time exploring every detail of the coal station among other parts of the base.

Main conveyor belt & tower on rails ("The Tower")

/images/loring/coal_dump_drive_motor.jpg Tower drive motor /images/loring/coal_dump_tons_per_hour.jpg Control room, with "Tons / Hour" indicator /images/loring/coal_dump_counterweight.jpg Main belt tensioning weight /images/loring/coal_dump_belt_tensioner.jpg Tensioner

The most obvious part of the coal station is the tower with the two conveyer belt "arms" sticking off to the side. There is a long conveyer belt that goes in a loop from the pit inside the building under where the trains go, up through the tower, back down to the main set of rollers, to the far end where the drive motor is, through a tensioner, and back.

/images/loring/coal_dump_midpoint_connection.jpg Midpoint cable connections /images/loring/coal_dump_cable_reel.jpg One of the cable spools /images/loring/coal_dump_slipring_case_and_connections.jpg Slipring case

The tower itself moves along a railway so it can distribute coal from its arms long a large area. All of the electrical power and control cables going to the tower came up out of the ground halfway along the railway. These two cables (Brenden and I believe one was power and the other was control) are wound around two spools on the tower and lay along the ground as the tower moves. Each spool we determined to have a set of slip ring connectors to connect the rotating spool to fixed connectors on the tower.

Inside the building

/images/loring/coal_dump_train_mover_winch.jpg Winch to move train cars into position /images/loring/coal_dump_electrical_1.jpg Electrical /images/loring/coal_dump_electrical_2.jpg Electrical /images/loring/coal_dump_dust_blower.jpg Dust collection /images/loring/coal_dump_dust_collector.jpg Dust collection

Train tracks run through the building which has large rubber ribbons hanging over each end likely for keeping coal dust contained. There is also a dust collection system with huge blowers. Under the building is a large pit that the main belt goes into. There is a grate under the tracks for the train cars to dump coal, the grate was big enough a person could fit through (IIRC) which was kinda sketchy. In another room there was an access stairway into the pit which had water in it. Brenden started going down the stairs but he "didn't want to go spelunking". To move (probably individual train cars) into position over the pit there is a winch attached to a cable that goes far away from the building in both directions, around pulleys, and connects back to itself in a loop. This what we think was going on - we didn't find the ends and the cable was mostly barried. The cable would be able to pull train cars in either direction along the tracks.

The Bunkers

/images/loring/mine_shop_outside.jpg The "Mine Shop" bunker /images/loring/mine_shop_inside.jpg Inside the "Mine Shop" /images/loring/assembly_bunker_antichamber.jpg Assembly bunker entrance /images/loring/assembly_bunker_main_door.jpg Assembly bunker door /images/loring/assembly_bunker_inside_hall.jpg Inner hallway

There was a heavily guarded area to the northeast of the base with bunkers to store the nukes. About half of them are accessible by car and the other half are in the woods and gated off, although you could ride a bike around back there. Most of them look like industrial hobbit holes and you can go inside them. There are two other bunkers which look like regular buildings except the entire second stories are solid concrete with fake windows painted on them. One of them is a guard shack, which I have not been able to get in. The other is I believe the building where the nukes would be assembled before being moved down to the south end of the runway to be loaded onto airplanes.

Fuel Tanks

/images/loring/big_tank_bottom.jpg Jet fuel tank /images/loring/big_tanks_top.jpg View from top /images/loring/big_tank_level_guage.jpg Level guage /images/loring/pipes_in_woods.jpg Pipes to one of the tanks /images/loring/big_tanks_plumbing_shack.jpg Plumbing shed

Back in the woods there are 2 large jet fuel tanks. Brenden and I walked up the stairs on one of them. We were looking around and he was trying to figure out what a piece of plumbing was for that went up to the top. He correctly guessed it to be a cable going up and back down to a weight on the inside for a fluid level guage.
