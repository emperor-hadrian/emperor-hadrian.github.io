What is amateur radio

In the early days of radio, there were certain bands or blocks of the radio spectrum allocated for hobbyists to experiment on. These hobbyists are called amateur radio operators although we are commonly referred to as "hams" which originated as an insult because the millitary thought they were "ham-fisted" when sending morse code badly. Nowadays there is a lot more to do using radios then sending morese code (also called CW - Continuous Wave) although that is still used to this day. There are many digital protocols used on the air similar to early internet modems over phone lines. I personally have used mostly CW and voice.

Equipment

There is a whole iceberg of radio equipment that can be used depending on what modes and frequencies are being used. I have mostly spent my time using the lower end of the spectrum, HF, which goes up to about 30 MHz. Building your own equipment is a big part of amateur radio and is something that I try to do, currently I have built everything except the radio itself. All of my equipment is intended to be small and portable so I can take it camping or bikepacking.

  • Radio

    Most importantly the radio, I bought a kit from QRP Labs called the QMX+ which I had to solder and hand-wind a lot of toroids for. It can only transmit around 5 watts however it is well engineered and has a lot of features in its software. At some point I plan to built an amplifier for it.

  • Antenna Matcher

    This piece of equipment is best explained in its own post but is needed to match the load of an antenna to what the radio transmitter expects to "see".

  • 12.8V LiFePO4 Battery

    Used for camping, other off-grid activities, self explanatory

  • Antenna wires with loading coil, choke balun

    Wires can be setup in either a dipole or vertical-ground-plane configuration. Choke balun and loading coil explained in seperate post.
