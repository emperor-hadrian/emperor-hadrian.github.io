List of Books - Hadrian Ward

Book/SeriesFinished?TimeNotes
The Bob BooksProbablyNo ideaMy first book series when I learned to read
Inside Out & Back AgainYes2016?We read it at Sweettree. I don't remember much of it since it was so long ago and I didn't know anything about the vietnam war.
The Way Things Work by David MacaulayYesNo idea Childrens book describing every day machines. Cool mammoths.
The Way We Work by David MacaulayNoNo idea Similar to The Way Things Work. I used to love the diagrams of intestines and muscles and stuff. One day when I was little my mom took it away from me without explanation. Now I know that was because it explains about sex.
Harry PotterYes2017?I loved all the books, I even read the last two of them twice because they are so good. After harry potter I didn't read much because nothing else was as good.
Practical Electronics for InventorsMostly2017? Great electrical engineering book I still refer to to this day. Back then I didn't understand much of it and it had lots of scary math in the beginning. I skipped past all the scary math symbols which I now know to be integrals, which doesn't mean I'm not still scared of them. What I spent most of the time reading about was digital electronics. I remember when I figured out how binary numbers work!
The OdeysseyYes2018? I was forced to read it in middle school and its a giant poem so I hated it.
New York 2140 by Kim Stanley RobinsonYesSummer 2021 Future climate change sci-fi set in NYC (obv). I loved it. Combination of finance, technology, human stories. I remember when I started it we drove into Rockland to go sailing in the dory. I was glued to it all the way back home too.
The Ministry For the Future by Kim Stanley RobinsonYesLate 2021? My favorite book by the author. It takes a lot of patience to read since it switches between several seperate stories all happening around the same time. The main plot is about a part of the UN meant to represent the rights of future generations, which of course means they deal with climate change.
Red Moon by Kim Stanley RobinsonYesLate 2021? In the near future, the US and China fight over the moon. Not my favorite, lots of cultural and philosophy references that I didn't understand. Its still sci-fi though so lots of action and technology.
Red/Green/Blue Mars series by Kim Stanley RobinsonNo2022? Mars colonization. I stopped in either the first or 2nd book, probably from being in school I don't remember.
40/50/60 series by Kim Stanley RobinsonNo2023 It follows a scientist working for the NSF in the near future trying to solve climate change. The enemy is not physics or nature but mostly politics which makes it a bit too realistic. Also his main character is such a creep.
2001: A Space Odyssey by Arthur C. ClarkeYesIdk Considering it was written so long ago it is scientifically detailed (although we did land on the moon around the same time so I shouldn't be suprised). Later I saw the the movie which was so ass and didn't explain anything.
Peak by Roland SmithYesSchool year 2023-24 I would have liked it more if I wasn't forced to read it in cindy comp. Its a book about a young boy climbing Mt Everest.
Ender's Game series by Orson Scott CardNoSummer 2023 or 2024 When I first visited MSSM, I was interviewed by Mr Katsman who told me to read it. Great sci-fi book with a twist. I definitely finished the first book. I don't remember where I stopped in the series, probably whenever I had to go back to MSSM.
Project hail Mary by Andy WeirYesSummer 2025 My second favorite book of all-time. I read it before the movie, the movie was not as good of course.
Artemis by Andy WeirYesSummer 2025 The main character lives in a moon base and gets involed in secret plots and stuff. Lots of action and near disasters.
Seveneves by Neal StephensonYesSummer 2025 My favorite book of all-time. Set with present-day technology, how will humanity survive an apocalypse? An incredible amount of science went into making it extremely realistic. Its really sad at times but I loved it. It also features Amateur Radio, specifically CW.
