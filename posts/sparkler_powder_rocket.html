Sparkler Rocket - Hadrian Ward

WARNING: Some of the things I tried were a bit too dangerous, especially having a rocket on a string. In general everything should be securely mounted FACING DOWNWARD with water below to absorb projectiles. Make sure a fuse will give you enough time to run away.

Origin

Ages ago angus and I were at the rogers' house and we decided to scrape sparkler powder off the metal wire and collect it into a vertical roll of paper. We then lighted it on fire and it burned very bright for a few seconds to basically make a flare. A second version involved more powder and a toilet paper tube.

On july 4'th 2025 I made basically the same thing: A metal tube that was blocked on one end amd had a small hole in a wood cap on the other end. We shot it out of the potato cannon with the intention of having it shoot out flames while high in the air. We got the timing wrong twice and shot it too early and it only went off back on the ground or close to the ground. I should also mention that the fuse for the flare was an intact sparler sticking out the exhaust hole that we would light before dropping it into the cannon. The fuse has to be lit before being dropped into the cannon because having a fuse that faces down will not be lit by the explosion in the cannon, I tested this and it did not work.

During winter of 2025/6 I experimented with burning the sparkler powder inside a piece of metal pipe. One end was blocked and the other end had a metal cap with a much smaller hole (nozzle) than previous designs. The fuse was still an intact sparkler sticking out the nozzle. Nearly all of these blew up with the failure every time being the metal nozzle being ripped out of the pipe in one way or another. I kept iterating through stronger methods to hold the nozzle in. Eventually I had 2 thick nails going through 4 holes near the end of the aluminum pipe to hold the nozzle in place. Those nails ripped out of the aliminum. I also tried diluting the sparkler dust with sawdust which sort of worked but was hard to get right and usually caused it to also blow up.

Baffled design (fail)

Photo 1 & 3: Baffles with spacers, these all fit snugly inside the big tube.

Photo 2: It still went bang with too many baffles.

Clearly, making the rocket stronger to resist the sparkler powder burning too fast was a loosing battle and only made it more dangerous. Summer 2026 I came up with a new concept to instead embrace the explosions (no it wasn't project orion). I kept the basic design with a metal tube blocked on one end and a nozzle on the other. I added a series of baffles (washers) inside the pipe which were held apart by a series of sections of smaller pipe that fit inside the big pipe but kept the washers spaced mosty evenly. Each "chamber" between the washers was filled with sparkler powder and the fuse (intact sparkler) ran down the middle of the entire thing. The idea was to have each chamber of sparkler powder explode and excape through the hole down the middle of all the washers and out the nozzle. I tested it with just one then a few chambers and it stayed intact. However when I added too many chambers the entire thing still blew up as soon as the fuse got to the first chamber.

What I think is going on

The sparkler powder burns much faster when under pressure. When it is in a tube with just an open end it behaves like a flare, birning fast and bright, but not violently. However when in a more confined space there is a positive feedback loop increasing the pressure, burning faster, increasing pressure, etc. This means it is impossible to have a steadily burning chamber with high pressure (inside a rocket). The baffled design also failed because the explosion from the first chamber would also go back to deeper chambers as well as out the nozzle and creating bascially the same chain reaction.
